Project Overview

The RoboMasters Standard Robot was developed as part of a competitive robotics platform designed for fast-paced combat-style operation requiring precision movement, rapid targeting, and reliable subsystem integration under dynamic operating conditions.

The project emphasized modularity, maintainability, structural rigidity, and control system responsiveness. Development involved iterative CAD modeling, rapid prototyping, subsystem integration, mechanical testing, and collaborative engineering workflows across multiple robot systems.

The robot incorporated drivetrain assemblies, stabilization systems, electronic integration, and mechanical mounting structures optimized for performance, durability, and ease of maintenance during testing and competition preparation.

Engineering Contributions

  • Assisted in mechanical design and CAD modeling for structural and subsystem components using SolidWorks.
  • Contributed to gimbal stabilization development focused on reducing vibration and improving targeting consistency during movement.
  • Supported drivetrain and chassis integration efforts to improve mobility, weight distribution, and overall system reliability.
  • Participated in prototype assembly, subsystem fitment verification, and iterative mechanical refinement.
  • Worked alongside team members on embedded systems integration and control architecture implementation.
  • Assisted in testing, debugging, and performance evaluation throughout iterative development cycles.

Engineering Challenges

  • Managing vibration and maintaining gimbal stability during aggressive acceleration and directional changes.
  • Balancing structural rigidity with overall system weight limitations while preserving maneuverability.
  • Designing modular assemblies that simplified maintenance and subsystem replacement during testing and competition preparation.
  • Improving drivetrain durability and alignment consistency under repeated operational stress.
  • Coordinating mechanical integration with embedded electronics and control systems within constrained packaging space.

Design & Development Process

Development followed an iterative engineering workflow involving CAD modeling, prototype fabrication, subsystem testing, and refinement based on observed system performance and reliability.

Mechanical components were evaluated for manufacturability, assembly efficiency, structural integrity, and maintainability throughout multiple design revisions. Testing cycles were used to identify vibration sources, improve subsystem mounting, and optimize component placement for improved balance and accessibility.

The project strengthened collaborative engineering practices involving troubleshooting, design communication, rapid iteration, and systems-level problem solving.

Project Outcomes

  • Improved structural stability and subsystem rigidity through iterative chassis refinement.
  • Reduced vibration within stabilization assemblies, improving overall targeting consistency and control response.
  • Increased modularity for faster assembly, maintenance, and subsystem replacement.
  • Gained hands-on experience with collaborative robotics development, rapid engineering iteration, and systems integration workflows.
